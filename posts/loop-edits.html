Escape and commit: an Astra-level loop fix for 1% of the tokens

19 Nov 2026 9 min read
  • interpretability
  • reasoning
  • 64.1%accuracy at 8k tokens, Astra 45.3%
  • 71.9%accuracy at 32k tokens, Astra 65.6%
  • 111×fewer generated tokens to build the edit
  • 84 minon one RTX 4080, no frontier model

In brief

A reasoning loop is a trap the model has a tiny chance of leaving in each pass. Astra's edit raises that chance only while the model loops, and it also pushes the model to close its reasoning.

Building those two terms directly from the model's own traces beats Astra at 8,192 tokens (paired p = 0.004) and matches it at 32,768, with about 1% of the generated tokens.

Small reasoning models often fail by looping. They repeat a block of their own reasoning until the token budget runs out, and the answer never arrives. Two weeks ago, Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab showed that a frontier model, GPT-6 Astra, could fix this in Qwen3.5-0.8B by editing its weights directly, with no training. Astra changed 131,072 weights in two MLP matrices and took MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%. It needed 50 forward passes and about 20 hours to get there.

Several groups have since shown that the frontier model is not needed (AHA!, CLC-sel, a linear program). I asked a different question. Why does the edit work? The answer turned out to have two parts, and building an edit from both parts gives Astra-level accuracy for about 1% of the tokens Astra generated.

What the edit does

Astra's edit touches 64 columns of mlp.down_proj in layers 15 and 19. Each column is a neuron's write into the residual stream, and each one is replaced by itself minus six times its component along a single direction u. Fitted on the released weights, the factor is 6.000 on every column, between 5.991 and 6.007. A neuron that used to push along u with gain +1 now pushes with gain -5. The edit does not delete the push. It reverses it.

The 64 columns are not arbitrary. They are the neurons that write most along u while the model loops. They carry 78% of the MLP's write along u in loops at layer 15 and 90% at layer 19, and they write about five to seven times more during loops than during productive reasoning. So the reversed push is large exactly when the model loops and small otherwise. On the vocabulary, the loop side of u ranks list restarts and hesitation tokens ("...*", "but rather", "still"), and the other side ranks tokens that commit to content (" thus", " result", " yields").

A loop is a trap with an escape rate

In an established loop of the base model, every token is nearly certain: the mean log-probability is -0.0001 nats per token over periods of about 55 tokens. Leaving the loop requires one improbable token somewhere in a period, so the chance of escaping per period is a sum of very small terms. Measured over 24 real loops, the median is 0.4%. That means about 260 periods, roughly 14,000 tokens, before the model leaves, which is more than the budget.

Repetition also deepens the trap. Feeding the model two lines of its own reasoning repeated k times, the mean log-probability of the k-th copy goes from -0.76 for the first copy to -0.03 by the eighth. This is the self-reinforcement effect Xu et al. described in 2022. A loop becomes permanent with positive probability when the chances of escaping shrink fast enough to add up to a finite total. Astra's edit puts a floor under them. With the edit, the copy curve stops at about -0.17 nats per token instead of going to zero, and escape from the same established loops goes from 0.4% to 99% per period.

The same reading explains a sharp threshold in the dose. The edit lowers the margin of the loop token by an amount that grows with the dose, and the escape rate of a period is a sum of terms exponential in that margin. Escape should therefore switch on as a double exponential, the way a particle escapes over a barrier. Applying the same kind of edit at layers chosen from that criterion, the escape per period is 1.0% at a dose of 2, 23% at 3 and 97% at 4. The law fitted through the doses of 3 and 4 puts the threshold at 3.5. It gives 1.9% at a dose of 2, where 1.0% is measured, and full escape from 5 on, as measured.

Escaping is not concluding

An edit built only for escape removes as many loops as Astra's, down to 12.5% of 64 practice problems at an 8,192-token budget. Its accuracy is 35.9%, against 45.3% for Astra. The missing part shows up in what the traces do after they leave the loop. They dither, writing "Wait... So 23.00. But wait..." until the budget runs out.

Astra's edit does something the escape-only edit does not. It raises the probability of closing the reasoning, the </think> token at the start of a line, by about 3 nats late in a trace. The escape-only edit lowers it slightly. So I fitted a second term of the same form, pushing from the dithering state toward the state just before </think>. A scan over layers 12 to 23 picked layers 19 and 15. Those are Astra's layers, found without using its edit. In this reading, Astra's single edit carries a commit drift at those layers, and its escape effect comes through the same neurons.

With both terms, accuracy at the 8,192-token budget is 64.1% against Astra's 45.3%. On the same problems, 14 are solved only by the two-term edit and 2 only by Astra (exact McNemar p = 0.004). The commit term was fitted on traces from half of these problems. On the other half the result is the same: 62.5% against 43.8%.

At Astra's budget

Astra was evaluated with 32,768 tokens, where a slow model also has time to finish. I ran both edits on 32 practice problems never used anywhere in this work, 16 from GSM8K and 16 from MATH, graded with math-verify. The two-term edit reaches 71.9% and Astra 65.6%. On GSM8K both reach 75.0%. On MATH it is 68.8% against 56.3%.

Paired by problem, 6 are solved only by the two-term edit and 4 only by Astra. With 32 problems that difference is not significant (p = 0.75), so the honest reading is a match. The two-term edit loops less, 6% of traces against 22%, and uses 19% fewer tokens.

What it cost

Astra generated 92 million tokens before it froze its edit. The two-term edit needs 0.83 million: the base model's traces on 48 problems, and the escape-edited model's traces on 64. Everything else is teacher-forced reading. On one RTX 4080, the whole construction took about an hour and a half, with no frontier model and no gradients.

Limits

This is one model and one failure mode. The comparisons use practice problems, not the public benchmarks, and 32 problems at the long budget. That is enough to call the two edits level and not enough to rank them. The other recent edits, AHA! and CLC-sel, also report results above Astra's at 32,768 tokens, and I have not yet run them on the same problems.

The model behind the threshold treats the tokens of a period as independent, and teacher forcing measures escape from a fixed context, not the trajectory that follows. I also see a precursor before loops: at layer 11, the variance and the autocorrelation of the projection on the loop direction rise over the 2,000 tokens before a loop starts, as they do before a tipping point. Near repetition that my detector misses could produce the same rise, so I report it as an observation for now.
